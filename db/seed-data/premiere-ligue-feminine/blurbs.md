# Première Ligue Feminine Team Blurbs (2026 Season)

### OL Lyonnes
Nineteen titles in twenty years, and the sharpest review of the 2025-26 season came from their own supporters' site: "Au bout de l'ennui... la nuit!" Boredom, then nightfall. Lyon went 19-3-0 with a goal difference of plus-65, and visitors to the 59,186-seat Groupama Stadium turn up hoping only to keep the score respectable. The women's club is a separate company majority-owned by Michele Kang, which is why it sailed serenely through the financial wreckage that nearly dumped the men into Ligue 2. Barcelona beating them 4-0 in the Champions League final in Oslo was the one result all season that made neutrals happy.

### Paris FC
The other Paris club now belongs to the richest family in France. Bernard Arnault's holding company took control with Red Bull as a junior partner, and the stake keeps climbing toward roughly 85% by 2027. The football has kept pace: a first Champions League campaign and a run to the 2026 final, where Lyon took them apart 5-0. Nobody in France pretends this is a grassroots story. But if you want the sensation of a project working in real time, Charléty is where it's happening.

### Paris Saint-Germain
Nine points, gone. In December 2025 the league docked PSG after a Fleury complaint exposed a striker fielded without her international transfer certificate; three wins were rewritten as forfeits, the club called the sanction "totalement disproportionné" and boycotted the end-of-season awards in a sulk. That is modern PSG in one paragraph: Qatari money, a squad rebuilt every summer, and a Champions League campaign that ended 17th of 18 without a win. The Kheira Hamraoui affair still shadows the institution, with prosecutors having requested that Aminata Diallo and six others face trial over the 2021 attack, allegations Diallo denies. A league game at the training ground drew 684 people; a Lyon game at the Parc drew 20,489. Both numbers are true and only one is flattering.

### FC Nantes
While the men flirted with relegation, the women reached the playoffs for the first time in the club's history, prompting fans to ask whether the two teams could just swap stadiums next year. They scored 42 and shipped 34, which is exactly the ratio you want if you like watching football rather than watching a game management seminar. 17,491 came to La Beaujoire for Marseille in December, the best crowd ever recorded for a French top-flight women's match involving neither Lyon nor PSG, with the Brigade Loire invited in behind them. Then Marseille poached the coach. Still the warmest story in the division.

### FC Fleury 91
A club from Fleury-Mérogis, a commune better known for housing France's largest prison, finished fifth and knocked Lyon out of the Coupe de France on penalties two seasons earlier. It was also Fleury's complaint that cost PSG nine points, which is the small club's revenge fantasy made real. They are independent, not the appendage of some men's superclub, and they play under the motto "À cœur vaillant rien d'impossible." Their home, the Stade Robert-Bobin, was built for Francophone Games that never happened and spent decades nicknamed le stade de l'inutile; in December 2025 it was rebranded the Essonne Stadium, which is one way to bury a joke.

### RC Strasbourg Alsace
Owned by BlueCo, the same fund that runs Chelsea, so file them under portfolio asset if you like. The Meinau makes that hard to do cleanly: France 3 reports the ground has the most female crowd in French football at around 26%, built by the Femmes de Foot association and its women-only Kop'In section, and 13,613 turned out for PSG. On the pitch, seventh and 38 conceded. A club whose terrace is more interesting than its team, and knows it.

### Le Havre AC
Eighth, 25 scored, 45 conceded, survival secured and very little else. The Stade Océane holds 25,178 and a November league game against Dijon drew 473 of them, which tells you where Normandy's women's football sits in the national conversation. Nothing about the Ciel et Marine is glamorous or scandalous. They stayed up, which for a club this size is the whole ambition. Pick them if you want a season nobody will ask you about.

### Olympique de Marseille
On 28 March 2026 the Vélodrome held 35,713 for a women's league match, a French record, and Marseille lost it 1-2 to Montpellier. That is the club in miniature: enormous gesture, ordinary result. They came up in 2025, hired Corinne Diacre, finished ninth with a minus-18 goal difference, and then hired Nantes' coach for the follow-up. The Olympico against Lyon is back on the calendar now, though it currently functions as a scheduled beating. Enormous upside, no evidence yet.

### Montpellier HSC
President Laurent Nicollin announced "une baisse drastique du budget" and said he was done throwing money at "joueuses qui arrivent de je ne sais où" — players who come from who-knows-where. He cited crowds of 500 to 600 and a €3m bill, then sold the section to Crux Football, an outfit run by former New Zealand international Bex Smith that pitches women's teams as something other than a cost centre. This from a club that helped invent French women's football. La Paillade finished tenth after a thirteen-match winless run, and their one great night was winning at Marseille in front of 35,713 people who had come to watch someone else.

### RC Lens
Relegated on merit in eleventh, then handed their top-flight place back when Dijon's women's company went into liquidation. They conceded 50 goals in 22 games. They also drew 10,712 to Bollaert-Delelis on the opening day against PSG, a club record, having already set the second-division attendance record the year before. Sang et Or, #RCElles, a crowd that turns up regardless of the arithmetic. The support is three divisions better than the team.

### Toulouse FC (promoted)
As Toulouse OAC they won four French titles in a row between 1999 and 2002 and reached a European semi-final. Almost nobody remembers. RedBird Capital owns 85% of the modern club, the women play at the Terrain Brice Taton in front of about a thousand, and they came up as Seconde Ligue champions on 49 points. Selen Altunkulak and Louna Lapassouse scored roughly two-thirds of the goals between them, which is a promotion strategy right up until somebody marks them. Historic name, current minnow.

### US Saint-Malo (promoted)
An independent Breton club founded in 1994, up to the top flight for the first time, with the city throwing them a civic reception. Les Diablesses play at the Stade de Marville, capacity around 2,500, which PSG's own website described as a véritable chaudron. The reality of second-tier life: about 900 for the promotion clincher at home, and 38 people at an away game in Reims. They will be favourites for relegation from the opening whistle. Take them if you want the romance and can stomach the scorelines.
