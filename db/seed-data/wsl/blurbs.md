# Which Club Should You Support? 2026-27 Women's Super League

### Manchester City
Ten years without a title ended with a game to spare, and Andrée Jeglertz's first season in England brought a league and cup double. Bunny Shaw took a third straight Golden Boot with 21 goals while the defence conceded 19 all year. Rivals will tell you it was bought in Abu Dhabi, and the sportswashing charge sticks to the parent company whether or not it's fair on the women who play. The strange part is the crowds went the other way: total home attendance fell by nearly 11,000, and the Etihad derby dropped from 22,497 to 17,520. Champions playing to a quieter room.

### Arsenal
Arsenal beat Barcelona 1-0 in Lisbon to win the 2025 Champions League and have spent the time since behaving like the club that takes women's football more seriously than anyone else in England. Every home league game is at the Emirates, average crowd 33,808, and the 56,537 who came for Chelsea in November is the third-biggest the WSL has ever recorded. More than four in every ten people who watched a WSL match last season watched Arsenal. Rivals call them front-runners who discovered the women's team around the time it started winning things. Fourteen goals conceded in 22 games and they still lost the title, mostly by drawing.

### Chelsea
Six titles in a row ended, third place, and for the first time in six years they go into Champions League qualifying rather than straight through. The louder story is the accounting. Two days before the 2024 financial year closed, Chelsea sold their women's team to a BlueCo sister company for a reported £198.7m, and the men's business duly posted a £128.4m profit; analysts lined up to call the valuation fiction, one of them putting the ceiling at £30m. Emma Hayes had already gone to the USWNT, Sam Kerr said her goodbyes, Millie Bright retired, and after a 5-1 hiding at City, Sonia Bompastor said out loud that she'd be happy to leave. Dynasty over, and the thing people remember is the paperwork.

### Manchester United
United disbanded their women's team in 2005 and didn't put it back together until 2018, and not much since has shaken the feeling that it's an afterthought. When Carrington was refurbished the women were moved into a portable building. Ratcliffe and Brailsford skipped the 2024 Women's FA Cup final to watch the men instead. Mary Earps left for PSG on a free and got booed on her return to Old Trafford, which tells you roughly how that relationship ended. Fourth, no European football, and 4,325 at Leigh Sports Village on opening weekend.

### Tottenham Hotspur
Nobody expected 36 points. Martin Ho's first season gave Spurs a club record and a joint-best fifth, sealed with a last-minute winner at Brighton, and it was gloriously daft to watch along the way: 35 scored, 38 conceded, 7-3 up at Villa, 5-1 down to City. Negative goal difference in fifth place. Home is Brisbane Road, Leyton Orient's ground, where 6,788 saw a North London derby that Arsenal have owned for most of its existence. The Spursy joke is getting harder to land.

### London City Lionesses
The only club in the division with no men's team attached to it. Michele Kang bought the old Millwall splinter club in 2023 and put it into Kynisca alongside OL Lyonnes and the Washington Spirit; captain Kosovare Asllani called her a pioneer paving the way for the next generation. Everything the club earns stays with the women, which is the entire pitch and genuinely rare in England. Sixth on debut, having sacked Jocelyn Prêcheur in December anyway, with Daniëlle van de Donk and Nikita Parris arriving. Hayes Lane in Bromley holds 5,150 and they are building a crowd from scratch, 3,294 on average and 5,414 for Arsenal.

### Brighton & Hove Albion
Tony Bloom made his money reading numbers better than bookmakers did, and Brighton's women look like the product of that: no noise, no fortune to spend, and a first FA Cup final in the club's history. They lost it 4-0 to City at Wembley in front of 43,917, having beaten the same side 3-2 in the league earlier in the year. Chiamaka Nnadozie in goal is why several of those results happened at all. Home is Broadfield Stadium in Crawley, which is not Brighton, and holds barely 3,000 of them on an average weekend. Nobody dislikes Brighton, which in this league counts as an achievement.

### Everton
When the men left Goodison Park, the women moved in permanently, which makes it the largest ground in the country handed to a women's team, with about £1m spent getting it ready. It worked immediately. Home attendance rose 147.9% in a season, from 21,512 to 53,334. Goodison held 45,000 for Dick, Kerr Ladies back in 1920, a symmetry Evertonians are happy to point out to you. Eighth, 37 conceded, Brian Sørensen gone in February and Scott Phelan confirmed in June, so nobody is claiming the football matched the venue.

### Aston Villa
Twenty-eight scored, 48 conceded, beaten 7-3 at home by Spurs and 6-1 at City. Ninth place has rarely been this watchable. Off the pitch it reads worse: Villa sold 90% of the women's team to their own parent company and 10% to Marc Zahr of Blue Owl Capital at a valuation around £55m, which kept the men's side the right side of profit and sustainability rules. It was the second version of the trick Chelsea had already pulled and it landed just as badly. Villa Park hosts the home games, 7,944 came for Arsenal, and Kirsty Hanson scored 12 in a bad team.

### West Ham United
Twenty goals in 22 games. Not chaos, just grim. They conceded 45, finished tenth, and stayed up only because the league decided to get bigger. Rehanne Skinner went in December and Rita Guarino arrived from Inter to sort it out. Home is the Chigwell Construction Stadium in Dagenham, a Dagenham & Redbridge ground, and with the men going down amid protests against the owners there was no spare goodwill in the building.

### Liverpool
Twelve games without a win, the longest run in the division, plus a 9-1 evisceration by Chelsea in the League Cup. Marie Höbinger and Sophie Román Haug both went down with ACL injuries. Eleventh, 21 goals scored, and the standing complaint from Liverpool supporters is that FSG have never funded the women anywhere near the men; the team was relegated in 2020 while the men were winning the league. Anfield gets the derby, 11,904 for Everton, and the rest of the year happens in St Helens. Home attendance fell by nearly 19,000 across the season.

### Birmingham City
In 2021 the Birmingham players wrote an open letter about their conditions: physio access, the gym, the equipment they were given. They went down the following season, and the letter is still the first thing anyone brings up. The club coming back up looks nothing like that one. Tom Wagner's Knighthead owns it, Tom Brady is an investor, Garry Cook runs it and Hope Powell is technical director, and they won WSL 2 at The Valley on the final day through two Wilma Leidhammar goals, taking the title from Charlton on goal difference. Whether any of the new money actually reaches the women is the thing worth watching.

### Crystal Palace
Down after one season, straight back up. Jo Potter's side finished level on points with Birmingham and four behind on goal difference, hammering Portsmouth 6-1 on the final day to make certain of it. Woody Johnson bought the club from John Textor in July 2025, in large part because UEFA's multi-club rules made Textor's position untenable once the men won the FA Cup. Selhurst Park gets the big games and 6,796 came for Sunderland. With Charlton up too, south London has a top-flight derby for the first time in a very long while.

### Charlton Athletic
Sophie Whitehouse saved four penalties. Charlton beat Leicester on spot kicks at The Valley in front of 3,979 and went up after 18 years away, which is the best story English football produced last season. The Addicks were a real power once, FA Cup finalists in 2003 and league runners-up the year after. Then the men's club axed the women's team in 2007 to save money and they started again in the lower leagues. Ask a Doncaster Rovers Belles supporter how unusual that is in this country.
